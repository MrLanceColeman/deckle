One reading surface for four kinds of input

Deckle looks like a clean note with a miniature player at the bottom. Everything resolves onto the same readable page.

1 · Pasted Text & Selection

Offline text to speech

Paste notes, drafts, or articles and press Read. Scrub the timeline to jump to any sentence, adjust speed from 120 to 280 WPM, or press Control-Option-D in the direct edition to read selected text from any app.

2 · Screenshot & Live Region

Read a screenshot aloud — or watch it live

Drop an image, paste with ⌘V, capture a screen region once, or click Read live to keep watching a region you drag and speak new words as they change on screen. Recognition uses on-device Apple Vision and filters out photo/video noise.

3 · Dropped Audio & System Output

Play an audio file and transcribe it here

Drop an MP3, M4A, WAV, or AIFF file to transcribe it on-device while it plays, or just play the file. In Settings, the direct edition can also tap what is already playing out of your Mac’s speakers without touching the microphone.

4 · Web Pages & Export

Listen to a page and save M4A audio

Paste a URL to extract the article and hear it locally. If a site returns a browser check, Deckle never solves or bypasses it—you can finish the check yourself in a normal window you control. Save any reading as a standalone .m4a file.

What you are paying for vs. what is already free

macOS already ships with free speech tools. You should use them if highlighted text is all you need.

Already free on your Mac

macOS Speak Selection, Outloud, and SpeechDock remain free for text you can already highlight in another window.

The $19 wedge

Deckle puts pasted text, screenshot and live-region OCR, dropped audio transcription, web-page reading, a sentence timeline scrubber, and M4A export in one quiet window.

What stays on your Mac

Speech uses the voices installed on your Mac. Screenshots and audio are recognized on-device and are not uploaded. Fetching a link needs the network; speech and screenshots do not.
